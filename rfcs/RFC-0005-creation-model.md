## Summary

The Ironstone Creation Model describes how something goes from an idea to a thing that exists, is used and is kept. It names the kinds of work creation needs, which I call **energies**. Eight are intrinsic, controlled by the company: ideate, think, make, do, judge, decide, execute and archive. Four are extrinsic and outside its control: fans, skeptics, competition and the law. Feedback loops run across all twelve. Most people and most teams are strong in two or three of them. The model exists so you can see which energies are present, which are missing, and what has to happen next.

The list is a naming order, not an execution order. There is no fixed sequence in a model like this.

Creation starts before analysis. It starts when an idea, a question, a pressure or a possibility enters the system. For a for-profit enterprise, the goal is to get that idea to market.

## The energies

| Energy | What it does | Its quantum: enough to tap another energy |
|---|---|---|
| **Ideate** | Creates ideas: options not yet judged. Generates them without killing them. Ideators observe, mix observation with memory, and frame ideas in words or pictures that others can use | There are more options than feel comfortable, and some are strange |
| **Think** | Turns ideas into systems: what the parts are, how they connect, what goes in and what comes out. Writes down every "this only works if" and ranks the assumptions by what kills you when they're wrong | A system someone could build from. That bar is lower than certainty |
| **Make** | Uses the system to create its inputs and outputs: a prototype, a draft, a model, the first real product | A small thing exists that could prove you wrong |
| **Do** | Runs the system, again and again, until it has a repeatable motion | It has run for real, more than once, not as a demonstration |
| **Judge** | Judges the results against the intent. Its output is opportunities: to change how something is thought about, made or done in the system. Can judge any part of the work, including the believers and the critics | A judgment, with its opportunities, that someone other than the maker would sign |
| **Decide** | Chooses which opportunities to act on and which direction to take: go left, go right, adopt, abandon or run the loop again | The choice is written down with its reason |
| **Execute** | Orchestrates everything: sets priorities, timing and resources across the other energies, and brings the work to market | The energies are coordinated and the work is in front of customers. If strangers can see it, it's production |
| **Archive** | Keeps the work, the decision and the lesson, and keeps them retrievable by every other energy | A later reader could pick it up without asking you |

## Energies and quanta, not stages

There is no order of execution. Each energy works until it reaches a **quantum**: a whole, usable unit of its own output, like a set of options worth thinking about, a prototype that could prove you wrong, or a decision written down with its reason. Below that threshold the energy has nothing to hand on. Once it reaches one, it can tap any other energy, in any direction.

So the model is a graph: energies are nodes, and taps between them are edges. Several energies can be live at once, at different altitudes. You can be in Do on the project while one deliverable is still in Make.

- **High-value taps.** Archive to Ideate, mining your own finished loops, is the most valuable and the least used. Judge to Think means the review found a strategy error, so re-plan instead of working harder. Do to Make means real use exposed a design flaw, so prototype the fix instead of patching it live.
- **Taps without a quantum.** Ideate straight to Execute is enthusiasm with a deadline: nothing was thought through or made. Execute to Ideate at 90% abandons a nearly finished quantum for a shinier idea.
- **Loops that never reach a quantum.** Think to Think is planning as procrastination. Make to Make is prototype addiction, usually fear of exposure.
- **The test for any tap.** Can you name the quantum you're carrying, or the specific thing you learned? If yes, it's a real move. If you're just uncomfortable, it's a retreat.

## Parallel energies and the messages between them

The work moves like this. Ideation creates ideas: options not yet judged. Thinking turns ideas into systems. Making uses those systems to create inputs and outputs. Doing runs the system until it has a repetitive motion. Judging assesses the results, and its output is opportunities to change how something is done, made or thought about in the system. Deciding picks which opportunities to act on. Archiving keeps all of it. **Execution orchestrates everything.**

That's a description of the flow, not an order of execution. All eight energies run at the same time. At any moment each one has two readings:

- **Level of energy:** how much attention and effort it's getting right now, from none to full.
- **Level of done:** how close it is to its next quantum.

A healthy venture has every energy live at some level. A stalled one usually has one or two at zero, and that's where to look first.

The energies coordinate by passing messages. Each one **demands inputs** it needs from the others and **gives outputs**, its quanta, to whichever energy needs them. Some typical messages:

| From | To | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Ideate | Think | Output: ideas worth building a system around |
| Make | Think | Demand: how should these parts connect? |
| Think | Make | Output: a system, with its inputs and outputs named |
| Make | Do | Output: a working version to run |
| Do | Judge | Output: results from running it again and again |
| Judge | Think, Make or Do | Output: opportunities to change how it's thought about, made or done |
| Judge | Decide | Output: the opportunities, with a verdict someone other than the maker would sign |
| Decide | Execute | Output: which opportunities to act on, with scope and date |
| Execute | All energies | Orchestration: what runs now, at what level of energy, with what resources |
| Any energy | Archive | Output: what it just made, ran, judged or decided, and what it learned |
| Archive | Any energy | Output, on demand: what was tried before, what happened, and why |
| Archive | Ideate | Output: the lessons of the last loop |

Reading a team this way, energy by energy and message by message, shows what's blocking the idea from reaching market: an energy with no effort on it, a demand nobody is answering, or an output nobody picked up.

## Every energy needs retrievable memory

Memory isn't only Archive's job. Every energy has to be able to retrieve what came before and to write down what it learns, or it starts from nothing each time. Ideate retrieves old ideas and the reasons they were dropped. Think retrieves the systems already built and the assumptions that failed. Make and Do retrieve the last version and how it ran. Judge retrieves past verdicts, so the same flaw isn't judged twice. Decide retrieves past decisions and their reasons. Execute retrieves the state of every energy, which is what makes orchestration possible.

Archive is the energy that keeps all of that retrievable. The test is retrieval, not storage: if an energy can't find what it needs when it needs it, the memory doesn't exist. This is the job NeoLibrary is built to do ([RFC-0001](RFC-0001-definitions.md)): every knowledge object keeps its sources, so any energy can find it and trust it.

## Recursion

The whole model runs again inside any single energy, governed by that energy. Inside Archive you can ideate (what could preservation look like here), think (which approach, for whose future use), make (one archive entry), do (run it across several projects), and so on. The parent energy sets the subject and the success test for the loop inside it. Two levels of recursion is plenty.

## Intrinsic and extrinsic energies

The model has twelve energies in two groups.

**The eight intrinsic energies** are the foundation: ideate, think, make, do, judge, decide, execute and archive. A company controls them. It decides how much effort each one gets, who carries it and what it produces.

**The four extrinsic energies** come from outside, and a company doesn't control them:

| Extrinsic energy | What it brings |
|---|---|
| **Fans** | Belief, adoption, word of mouth, early demand. They pull the work forward |
| **Skeptics** | Critics and doubters. They test whether the work holds up |
| **Competition** | Rivals and substitutes chasing the same customers, talent, attention and money. They set the bar and the clock |
| **The law** | Regulation, contracts, rights and obligations. It sets what the work is allowed to be |

**Feedback loops run across all twelve.** Fans' demand feeds Ideate and Execute. A skeptic's attack feeds Judge and Think. A competitor's move feeds Think (how you're positioned), Judge (what good now looks like) and Execute (when to move and at what price). A new rule feeds Think and Decide, and can stop Execute outright. The intrinsic energies push back the other way: what Execute ships creates fans, skeptics and a competitive response, and what Archive keeps is the record the law will ask for. A company can't control the extrinsic energies, but it can listen to them, prepare for them and design for them.

That's why the internal skeptic matters. Judging honestly inside the team, before strangers do it, is what makes the outside skeptics survivable. In the NeoLibrary repos the internal skeptic is an aspect that wraps every intrinsic energy ([RFC-0004](RFC-0004-how-we-build.md)).

## Roles

An energy is often carried by a person, and it's rare for one person to carry all of them. The judge in particular needs distance from the work: it's hard to judge objectively what you created. In small companies the judge is often an outside advisor, trusted for exactly that distance. The judging must not kill the energy of the creative work.

## Where AI fits

AI can take part in every energy, and the model was framed from the start as a framework for human and machine collaboration. What changes:

- Generating ideas and fluent strategy prose now costs nothing, so fluency is no longer evidence of clear thinking. Grade the assumptions, not the prose (see [RFC-0007](RFC-0007-evidence-ladder.md)).
- Rough versions now look finished, which makes Make and Judge harder, not easier.
- An archive is now queryable working context. Archives used to be write-only because retrieval cost more than rewriting from memory. That has flipped, which makes Archive the energy that compounds.

The person leads; the tools amplify.

## What I've built with it

**NeoLibrary.** I used this model to create NeoLibrary, and the NeoLibrary repos are still governed by it ([RFC-0004](RFC-0004-how-we-build.md)).

**Prompt-based AI agents.** It's also how I build AI agents from prompts. I saw this coming in July 2023, when I said the model's "definitions and role descriptions" could be used as prompt sequences for AI tools, to generate ideas, systems and useful products and services. In an agent built this way, the parts of the model map directly:

| In the model | In a prompt-based agent |
|---|---|
| An energy | A role, with its own prompt: what it does and what it produces |
| A quantum | The output the role must produce before anything is handed on |
| Demands and outputs | The messages between roles: what each one asks for and returns |
| Retrievable memory | A shared store every role reads before it starts and writes to when it's done |
| Execute | The orchestrator that decides which role runs, when, and with what inputs |
| The skeptic | A critic role called into any step, never left to grade its own work |

## Why eight: a model that reproduces itself

The model started in 2022 with fewer energies. I added Execute and Archive later, for two reasons. Without Execute, nothing reaches the people it was made for. Without Archive, every loop starts from nothing. Archive is what makes the model reproduce itself: a finished loop, written down, becomes the raw material for the next Ideate, so each cycle seeds the one after it.

## Scope

The model was designed for groups: in particular the first 20 people in an organization taking digital ideas to their first few million dollars of revenue. It also works for one person moving between energies. Physical goods follow a different model.
